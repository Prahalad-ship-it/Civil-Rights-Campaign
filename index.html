About the Center

Indian Law Resource Center

The Indian Law Resource Center is a nonprofit law and advocacy organization established and directed by Indigenous people with the main goal of preserving the well-being of Indian and other Native nations or tribes by providing free legal assistance. Founded in 1978 by Robert T. Coulter to confront the glaring gap in justice for Indigenous communities across America, which had no public interest organization willing to fight for their rights in court.

Their work spans from protecting Indigenous lands and natural resources, defending human rights and cultural heritage, ending violence against Native women, and advocating for environmental justice across the Americas. They played a leading role in securing the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples.

Today, the center operates across 4 regions around the world with 29 ongoing projects after 45 years of service. Domestically it works with the National Congress of American Indians and the National Indigenous Women's Resource Center. Internationally, it collaborates with COIAB, APIB, and AIDSEEP. They also hold NGO consultative status with the United Nations Economic and Social Council.

Acerca del Centro

Centro de Recursos

El Centro de Recursos de Derecho Indígena es una organización sin fines de lucro fundada en 1978 por Robert T. Coulter para brindar asistencia legal gratuita a los pueblos indígenas de las Américas. Su trabajo abarca la protección de las tierras indígenas, los recursos naturales, los derechos humanos y el patrimonio cultural.

Desempeñó un papel fundamental en la aprobación de la Declaración de la ONU sobre los Derechos de los Pueblos Indígenas. Hoy el Centro opera en 4 regiones del mundo con 29 proyectos en curso, brindando asistencia legal gratuita a todos los pueblos indígenas de las Américas.

Crisis · VAWA 2013 · Safe Women Strong Nation

Advocacy Efforts

The Indian Law Resource Center addresses one of the overlooked human rights crises in the United States — violence against Native Women in tribal lands and Alaska Native Villages. More than 4 in 5 American Indian and Alaska Native Women experienced violence in their lifetime, and more than 1 in 2 have experienced sexual violence.

For more than 35 years, US law stripped Indian nations of criminal authority over non-Indians — who reportedly commit 96% of sexual violence against Native Women. Between 2005 and 2009, US attorneys refused to prosecute 67% of Indian country matters involving sexual abuse.

The Center fought back and secured the 2013 reauthorization of the Violence Against Women Act — allowing tribal nations to prosecute non-Native offenders for the first time. They also brought the crisis before the United Nations and the Organization of American States.

"4 in 5 Native women experience violence in their lifetime. 96% of that violence goes uncharged." Indian Law Resource Center · 2026

Crisis · VAWA · Mujeres Seguras

Esfuerzos de Defensa

El Centro aborda una de las crisis de derechos humanos más ignoradas en los Estados Unidos: la violencia contra las mujeres nativas. Más de 4 de cada 5 mujeres indígenas experimentaron violencia en su vida, y más de 1 de cada 2 sufrieron violencia sexual.

Durante más de 35 años, la ley de EE. UU. privó a las naciones tribales de autoridad criminal sobre los no indígenas, quienes cometen el 96% de la violencia sexual contra las mujeres nativas. El Centro aseguró en 2013 la reautorización de la Ley de Violencia contra la Mujer (VAWA), una victoria histórica.

Crise · VAWA · Femmes en Sécurité

Efforts de Plaidoyer

Le Centre s'attaque à l'une des crises des droits humains les plus négligées aux États-Unis : la violence contre les femmes autochtones. Plus de 4 femmes autochtones sur 5 ont subi des violences au cours de leur vie. Pendant plus de 35 ans, la loi américaine a privé les nations tribales de toute autorité pénale sur les non-Amérindiens.

Le Centre a obtenu en 2013 la réautorisation de la loi VAWA, permettant pour la première fois aux nations tribales de poursuivre les agresseurs non autochtones.

Safe Women · Strong Nation · MMIW · 229 Alaska Native Tribes

Tackling the Problem

The Center's Safe Women, Strong Nation project partners with Native women's organizations and Alaska Native nations to end violence against Native women and girls across America by providing free legal assistance, policy advocacy, and on-the-ground support to communities that have been failed by the legal system for decades.

What the Safe Women, Strong Nation project does:

  • Provide free legal advice to Native women's organizations and tribal nations.
  • Bring the crisis to the United Nations and Organization of American States.
  • Restore tribal criminal authority to prosecute non-Native offenders on tribal lands.
  • Train tribal law enforcement, courts, and prosecutors.
  • Help tribal nations create domestic violence codes and justice systems.
  • Fight for the 229 Alaska Native tribes previously excluded from federal protection.
  • Raise awareness for missing and murdered Indigenous women (MMIW).

Mujeres Seguras · Nación Fuerte · MMIW

Enfrentando el Problema

El proyecto "Mujeres Seguras, Nación Fuerte" se asocia con organizaciones de mujeres nativas y naciones nativas de Alaska para brindar asistencia legal gratuita y apoyo directo a las comunidades.

Qué hace el proyecto:

  • Proporciona asesoría legal gratuita a organizaciones de mujeres nativas.
  • Lleva la crisis ante la ONU y la OEA para ejercer presión global.
  • Restaura la autoridad criminal tribal para enjuiciar a agresores no nativos.
  • Capacita a tribunales tribales, fiscales y fuerzas del orden.
  • Ayuda a las naciones tribales a crear códigos de violencia doméstica.
  • Lucha por las 229 tribus nativas de Alaska excluidas de la protección federal.
  • Concientiza sobre las mujeres indígenas desaparecidas y asesinadas (MMIW).

समस्या का समाधान

सेंटर का "सुरक्षित महिलाएं, मजबूत राष्ट्र" प्रोजेक्ट मूल महिला संगठनों और अलास्का मूल राष्ट्रों के साथ साझेदारी करता है, मुफ्त कानूनी सहायता और नीति वकालत प्रदान करता है।

प्रोजेक्ट क्या करता है:

  • मूल महिला संगठनों और आदिवासी राष्ट्रों को मुफ्त कानूनी सलाह प्रदान करना।
  • संयुक्त राष्ट्र और अमेरिकी राज्यों के संगठन में संकट उठाना।
  • आदिवासी भूमि पर गैर-मूल अपराधियों पर मुकदमा चलाने के लिए आदिवासी आपराधिक अधिकार बहाल करना।
  • 229 अलास्का मूल जनजातियों के लिए लड़ना।
  • लापता और हत्या की गई स्वदेशी महिलाओं (MMIW) के लिए जागरूकता बढ़ाना।

Femmes Sécurisées · Nation Forte · MMIW

Faire Face au Problème

Le projet "Femmes en sécurité, Nation forte" s'associe aux organisations de femmes autochtones et aux nations autochtones d'Alaska pour mettre fin à la violence grâce à une assistance juridique gratuite et un soutien sur le terrain.

Ce que fait le projet :

  • Fournir des conseils juridiques gratuits aux organisations de femmes autochtones.
  • Porter la crise devant l'ONU et l'Organisation des États américains.
  • Rétablir l'autorité pénale tribale pour poursuivre les agresseurs non autochtones.
  • Former les forces de l'ordre tribales, les tribunaux et les procureurs.
  • Défendre les 229 tribus autochtones d'Alaska exclues de la protection fédérale.
  • Sensibiliser aux femmes autochtones disparues et assassinées (MMIW).

Not Your Stories

By Srinaga Prahalad Pillutla  ·  #USvsHate  ·  Global Code Catalyst

They see my face and think they know my story
They believe they've read me without turning the page
A quiet, smart, and humorless Asian boxed in a single frame
A background noise in the halls where I am never seen or heard
They joke about my name, history, and culture
Like they would know the struggle of a person
Or the sleepless nights knowing I would not visit my family
Or how I eat food alone in the stinky school bathroom
"Go back to your home, you stinky Asian," they say
But where should I go, to your home or to your grandmother's house
Because this is the place where I made a comfortable home with huge dreams.
What many people realize is that my roots stretch as far as the ocean
Yet, the voice, the passion, the dream, and the freedom were raised here
Whether it be in the school, home, or the country, it has given me opportunities

So I am not your stereotype to you or others or a Trend or a Punchline
Not a "Model Myth Minority" to divide us and pretend we won't notice
Asian Americans are not people who can be written off based on their faces

We are not a joke to you or others
We are not an ethnicity that you can laugh at
We are not who you can assume based on our physical identity.
We are an ethnicity that has countless languages that people speak
We share countless stories in one shared space
We carry strength in silence
We carry the dream and achieve the dream
We are leaders, artists, dreamers
We stand together, louder and stronger
And we are part of this nation's history in the past and the future
Say no to xenophobia and stereotypes that don't represent us
Say no to hate crimes against Asian Americans
Stand with us in fighting hate and racism
Because nobody can divide others if we stand together
"We carry strength in silence" — Not Your Stories, Srinaga Prahalad Pillutla

Background Story Historia de Fondo Tlahtoltzintli पृष्ठभूमि की कहानी Histoire de Fond

This poem was based on my experiences in eighth grade. When I joined public school after attending online school, I began to understand what it felt like to be judged for my identity. During that time, I faced a lot of comments about my culture, my name, and my identity, my food, and I treated it like a joke, and to this day, it stays with me, even if I am in high school, still thinking about those moments. There were days when I felt like an outsider in my Middle School, especially since I looked different and was judged based on my skin color or the food my mom packed every day. What hurt the most were the words and assumptions, and how people define others based on their identity or their culture. However, as I started to become more mature, I realised that my identity is shaped by my parents' belief in hard work and success, my culture, and growing up in both India and America — and my identity is my strength, not something to be mocked. I wrote this poem to hopefully inspire others to speak up against hate, xenophobia, and false stereotypes that could affect an entire community.

Este poema se basa en mis experiencias en octavo grado. Cuando me uní a la escuela pública, comencé a entender lo que se siente cuando te juzgan por tu identidad. Recibí muchos comentarios sobre mi cultura, mi nombre y mi comida. He aprendido que mi identidad es mi fortaleza. Escribí este poema para inspirar a otros a hablar contra el odio, la xenofobia y los estereotipos falsos.

In xochitlahtolli omochih ipan notequiuh itech chicunauhtetl xihuitl. Niquin nochi ixco in masehualme, oquimatque tlen quichihua in teixcuitiani. In xochitlahtolli omochih itech nopan, inin niquinpalehuia in masehualme itech tlahtlacoa ihuan teixcuitiani.

यह कविता आठवीं कक्षा में मेरे अनुभवों पर आधारित है। मैंने सीखा कि मेरी पहचान मेरी ताकत है। मैंने यह कविता दूसरों को घृणा, ज़ेनोफोबिया और झूठे रूढ़िवादों के खिलाफ बोलने के लिए प्रेरित करने की उम्मीद से लिखी, क्योंकि दुनिया तब बेहतर होती है जब विविधता और स्वीकृति हो।

Ce poème est basé sur mes expériences en huitième année. J'ai appris que mon identité est ma force. J'ai écrit ce poème pour inspirer les autres à s'opposer à la haine, à la xénophobie et aux faux stéréotypes, car le monde est meilleur avec la diversité et l'acceptation.
